# Weekly SNS Roundup: 2026-07-19

## Executive signal

The strongest signal this week is **design before control**. New work on power allocation, fault-tolerant modular swarms, and long-lived swarm maintenance converges on the same warning: aggregate capability can look healthy while individual receivers starve, local failures cascade, or continuous control consumes the mission it is supposed to preserve.

For SNS, this means three things. First, every relay architecture needs explicit unserved-target and starvation metrics. Second, swarm geometry, topology, passive stability, and extensibility should be represented before adding more controller sophistication. Third, the active thermal-loss boundary must now be connected to evidence about degraded coatings and interfaces, not treated as a free numerical target.

`QST-STOR-0002` remains the canonical priority. This roundup proposes one refinement to its evidence checklist and does not enact a queue change.

## Evidence notes

### Materials / PV

**No qualification-changing PV result identified.**

- **Fact:** NASA's 2026 SPRAT program explicitly includes perovskites, tandems, blankets, PMAD, environmental interactions, qualification, and laser-power-beaming photovoltaics, showing that these remain active space-PV research categories rather than settled technologies.
- **Fact:** An ESA Open Space Innovation Platform entry proposes radiation-tolerant CsPbBr3 perovskite cells and cites proton-irradiation stability, but it is a community proposal rather than an independently verified flight or combined-stressor qualification result.
- **Inference:** The repository should hold PV readiness steady. Radiation tolerance in a material film does not close encapsulation, interfaces, vacuum, thermal cycling, atomic oxygen, contamination, deployment, or end-of-life power uncertainty.
- **Speculation:** Perovskites may ultimately serve role-specialized or replaceable surfaces better than universal long-life node skins.
- **Sources:** https://www.nasa.gov/glenn/sprat/ ; https://ideas.esa.int/core/servlet/hype/IMT?documentId=959883d2fa2807cbeded0101d4b0a456&templateName=&userAction=Browse
- **Tags:** [PV][MAT][MFG]

### Storage / thermal control

**The active thermal-loss result remains controlling.**

- **Fact:** No new external source found this week invalidates the `QST-STOR-0002` conclusion that the declared 10 mm node requires approximately effective emissivity <= 0.10 and parasitic conductance <= 5e-5 W/K in the favorable 30-minute case.
- **Inference:** The next useful evidence work is not another unconstrained storage mechanism. It is a qualification-style checklist connecting candidate coatings, supports, wires, adhesives, PCM packaging, and contamination states to the required loss boundary.
- **Speculation:** A credible survivor may require role specialization, deployable insulation, warmer operating thresholds, shorter eclipse exposure, or cluster-level thermal support rather than a monolithic universal seed.
- **Tags:** [STOR][CTRL][MAT]

### Metasurfaces / beaming

**NRL dual-use long-distance laser system demonstration, June 17, 2026.**

- **Fact:** The U.S. Naval Research Laboratory reported a laser system that transmitted power over long distance and rapidly changed mission mode during a terrestrial demonstration with Boeing and the U.S. Army.
- **Inference:** Fast switching, tracking, command authority, and receiver-state management belong in the SNS beam-control abstraction alongside optical efficiency. The demonstration strengthens the terrestrial technology ladder but does not establish orbital power-transfer efficiency or thermal closure.
- **Speculation:** A future SNS relay may share apertures or control infrastructure across communication, sensing, and power delivery, but this creates safety and scheduling conflicts that must be modeled explicitly.
- **Source:** https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/display-news/Article/4519573/nrl-demonstrates-dual-use-laser-system-for-power-beaming-and-counter-uas-operat/
- **Tags:** [META][SBSP][CTRL]

### Swarm / autonomy

**Fault-tolerant modular robotic swarm for on-orbit assembly, published June 10, 2026.**

- **Fact:** Qi et al. propose cooperative reconfiguration that preserves manipulation capability after joint failures without relying on spare replacement modules.
- **Inference:** SNS should represent local role substitution, topology changes, and capability degradation rather than binary node survival alone.
- **Speculation:** Modular reconfiguration concepts may transfer more readily to larger relay or assembly nodes than to 10 mm seeds.
- **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1088/2631-7990/ae6bde
- **Tags:** [SWARM][CTRL][MFG]

**Design-first maintenance of large spacecraft swarms, published June 25, 2026.**

- **Fact:** Xu argues that perpetual swarm maintenance must be low-frequency and that deployment cost, extensibility, configuration design, and collective safety should be first-class design objectives.
- **Inference:** Before optimizing a sophisticated controller, SNS simulations should ask whether geometry and passive configuration can reduce required maneuver arcs and pairwise safety checks.
- **Speculation:** The most scalable SNS architecture may look less like continuously choreographed particles and more like families of passively favorable orbital structures with sparse correction events.
- **Source:** https://doi.org/10.62762/AEC.2026.392649
- **Tags:** [SWARM][CTRL]

### Asteroids / ARCI

- **Inference:** Better swarm maintenance or power allocation can improve survey execution without increasing confidence that a target contains valuable material.
- **Speculation:** ARCI may eventually need a separate operations-confidence dimension so improved survey reach does not leak into resource-confidence scores.
- **Tags:** [ARCI][SWARM]

### SBSP / ecosystem

**Constellation-Level Power Allocation for LEO SBSP, arXiv:2606.18416, June 16, 2026.**

- **Fact:** The authors simulate a 20-satellite Walker constellation serving eight ground stations while modeling eclipse cycles, microwave coupling, atmospheric attenuation, and onboard batteries. Their greedy policy leaves two sites unserved during the run despite substantial peak aggregate delivery.
- **Inference:** Aggregate megawatts are an inadequate success metric. SNS relay models need delivered-energy distribution, longest unserved interval, coverage probability, and fairness or service guarantees.
- **Speculation:** A heterogeneous SNS power network may need market, mission-priority, or safety policies that deliberately trade peak throughput for bounded starvation.
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18416
- **Tags:** [SBSP][CTRL][SWARM][STOR]

## Longitudinal clusters

1. **Power networks are allocation systems, not pipes.** Recent SBSP work joins earlier relay studies in showing that orbital geometry, batteries, link availability, and policy jointly decide who receives energy.
2. **Swarm scaling moves pressure into architecture.** Resource-aware scheduling, fault-tolerant reconfiguration, and design-first maintenance all suggest that topology and passive structure can dominate controller cleverness.
3. **Thermal credibility now depends on evidence quality.** `QST-STOR-0002` has produced a measurable boundary; the remaining challenge is whether candidate material and interface stacks can meet it after environmental degradation.
4. **PV remains promising but qualification-bound.** New programs and proposals sustain research relevance without closing the flight-readiness gap.

## Contradictions and negative evidence

- A high aggregate SBSP delivery result can coexist with permanently unserved receivers. This weakens any evaluation based only on total or peak delivered power.
- More active swarm control can consume payload time, maneuver budget, communication capacity, and safety-analysis effort. Controller complexity is not free resilience.
- Material-level proton tolerance does not equal a flight-qualified photovoltaic stack.
- The current thermal boundary is a system target, not proof that a coating or support interface can meet it after environmental exposure.

## Quest consequences

- **Refine `QST-STOR-0002`, do not replace it.** Add environmental qualification columns to the planned thermal-loss evidence checklist. This follows the canonical move and prevents nominal material constants from masquerading as packaged performance.
- **Refine `QST-SIM-0003` later.** Add unserved-target, starvation-duration, and fairness metrics before interpreting aggregate relay power.
- **Refine `QST-SIM-0002` later.** Add design-first maintenance variables before increasing controller complexity.
- **No new quest.** The active queue is already at its eight-quest ceiling, and the evidence fits existing work.
